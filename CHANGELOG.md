# Changelog

All notable changes to this project are listed here.

## Unreleased

### Added

- Web app shell: Vite, React and strict TypeScript, with the routes `/`, `/judge`, `/measured`, `/rules`, `/ran`, `/about`, `/worksheet` and `/blind/:token`. The `/desk/listing` and `/desk/eval` routes exist in development builds only. Verified by `src/App.test.tsx`.
- `dist/version.json` with the commit SHA and build time, written at build.
- `scripts/tone-check.ts`: fails on long dashes and curly quotes in user-facing text, and fails when it walks fewer files than its floor. Verified by `scripts/tone-check.test.ts`.
- `scripts/model-guard.ts`: fails on banned browser AI APIs and model libraries, on `availability(` outside `src/ai/gate.ts`, and on `getGenerativeModel` outside `src/ai/firebaseModels.ts`, with a floor on files walked. Verified by `scripts/model-guard.test.ts`.
- CI on GitHub Actions (`ubuntu-24.04`): lint, type check, tests, build, tone check, model guard, gitleaks over full history, and a check that no commit message carries a co-author trailer.
- Apache-2.0 `LICENSE` and `NOTICE`.
- `src/shared/itemRead.ts`: the item-read contract, 16 required fields and 37 categories from one field table, which also builds the strict JSON Schema and the parser. The parser rejects non-JSON, missing or extra fields, wrong types, unknown enum values and negative numbers, names the bad field, and clips label text to 160 characters. Verified by `src/shared/itemRead.test.ts`.
- `src/engine/types.ts` and `src/engine/edition.ts`: rules engine types and a strict, hashed rule edition loader. Unknown fields on rules and destination lines are refused, so no field holding USPS wording can enter. The Wh tolerance must stay below the measured 3.8% misread. The hash ignores key order. `scripts/build-edition.ts` assembles `rules/edition.json` from the part files with an id taken from its content; `--check` runs in CI and fails on a stale edition. Limits re-read at Pub 52 622.5 on 2026-11-17: 4 cells or 2 batteries, not both; cell 20 Wh and 1 g; battery 100 Wh and 2 g. Suspended destinations (12) read from the USPS service alerts page, updated 2026-11-06. Verified by `src/engine/edition.test.ts`.
- `src/engine/battery.ts`: battery facts from the printed numbers. Our code computes Wh = V x mAh / 1000 and compares it with the printed Wh; a difference above the edition tolerance is a mismatch, and the printed Wh is then not used. The measured misread (2500 mAh read beside 9.62 Wh, 3.8% off) is a mismatch at 3%. Volts outside 1 to 60, mAh outside 10 to 100,000 or Wh outside 0.05 to 400 mark the read implausible. A battery counts as a cell only when the sender says one cell; above 5 V or above 20 Wh it counts as a battery; otherwise it stays unknown. Verified by `src/engine/battery.test.ts`.
- `src/engine/facts.ts` and `rules/label-words.json`: item facts from the read, the sender's fixes, answers and label words. Label words match whole words in any case; "alcohol-free" and "non-flammable" are not read as hazards; a coin-cell code (CR and four digits) or "lithium metal" gives lithium metal. A `label_text` tag records that label text was read, so label identity survives a reload once the text stays on the laptop. A category alone never confirms identity. When the label and an answer disagree on alcohol, the stricter reading is kept. Verified by `src/engine/facts.test.ts`.
- `src/engine/lithiumBox.ts`: the per-box lithium rule from Pub 52 622.5, at most 4 cells or 2 batteries, not both. Only batteries installed in their device count. A device whose battery could be a cell or a battery is tried both ways, so the result is `ok`, `over`, `mixed` or `depends`, with the range of counts and the item ids. Quantity multiplies. Any text that points toward a second box carries the two-box limit. Verified by `src/engine/lithiumBox.test.ts`.
- `src/engine/predicates.ts`: evaluates rule predicates (`all`, `any`, `not`, and `eq`, `in`, `has`, `gt`, `lte` on dotted fact paths such as `battery.wh`). A missing or null fact never matches a positive test, so an unknown value cannot satisfy a rule by accident. Source: plan Task 11. Verified by `src/engine/predicates.test.ts`, which failed before the module existed.
- `src/engine/itemRules.ts`: turns each item-scope rule whose condition holds into a hit carrying its section, link, our wording and any question id. Box and note rules are left to the box check. Source: plan Task 11. Verified by `src/engine/itemRules.test.ts`, which failed before the module existed.
- `rules/usps-export.json`: the USPS export rules in our own words, each with its section and source link: flammable liquids and other flammable goods, aerosols, alcoholic drinks, matches and lighters, e-cigarettes, tobacco, cigars (check), mercury, hemp and CBD, dry ice, magnets (check), glue and paint (check); the lithium rules (loose or packed-beside batteries and power banks, used devices, opened new devices, no Wh rating, numbers that disagree, cells above 20 Wh, batteries above 100 Wh, lithium metal cells); the four box and note rules for the box check; and the data rules for an unsure read and the three questions. The power bank rule is marked as an inference. No USPS sentence is stored. Sources re-read live on 2026-11-17: Pub 52 622.5, 623, 349 and 424, Packaging Instructions 2A and 3C, IMM 136, and the USPS international shipping restrictions page. Verified by `tests/rules/usps-export.test.ts` (14 tests, including a floor of 25 rules and a check that the Wh limits in the rules equal the edition limits), which failed against the empty rule list first.
- `rules/destinations/CA.json`, `PH.json` and `index.json`: destination editions for Canada (35 lines) and the Philippines (12 lines) from the IMM edition of 2026-11-02, read in full on 2026-11-17. Every prohibition and restriction line is covered, plus the observations that bear on a gift box. Each line stores its sha256 (of the line text with whitespace collapsed), an id, our own wording and its tag matches; no listing text is stored, and no wording shares six or more words in a row with its source line. `PH-P-01` (liquids, easily liquefiable items, glass) takes out `liquid` and `glass_fragile` and checks `easily_liquefiable`. Canada has no liquids line, so the lotion vector holds. Customs notes are in our words, including Canada's gift card note. Source: plan Task 12, `https://pe.usps.com/text/imm/ps_006.htm` and `ce_003.htm`. Verified by `tests/rules/destinations.test.ts`, which failed before the edition was rebuilt.
- `src/engine/destination.ts`: matches a destination's listing lines against an item's tags, one hit per line at its most severe match, and drops a match when the sender gave its exception answer. A suspended country (`DEST-SUSPENDED`), a country we have not reviewed (`DATA-DEST-UNREVIEWED`) and a listing with no list (`DATA-DEST-NO-LIST`) each give "Check this". `tests/vectors/destinations.json` holds 15 named cases across Canada, the Philippines, an unknown country and a suspended one; `tests/engine/helpers.ts` holds the shared test helpers. Source: plan Task 12. Verified by `src/engine/destination.test.ts`, which failed before the module existed.
- `src/engine/layers.ts`: the four rule layers behind each verdict (USPS export rules, the destination's rules, service suspensions, the destination's customs), each with its edition and check date. A layer is stale when its check is older than the edition's limit (120 days for export rules, 60 for the destination and customs, 21 for suspensions); a country that is not reviewed or has no list is missing its rules and customs layers. Today's date comes from the caller. Source: plan Task 12. Verified by `src/engine/layers.test.ts` (including the day-60 and day-61 boundary), which failed before the module existed.
- `src/engine/verdict.ts`: the box verdict. Each item gets its facts, item rule hits, destination hits and the four layers; the lithium count runs over the items not already taken out and adds a box hit to each counted item (`over` takes out, `mixed` follows the edition setting, `depends` checks and asks the cell question on the unknown items). An item is "OK to pack" only with no check or take-out hit left: identity has evidence (else `DATA-IDENTITY`), the read is sure, every question is answered, and all four layers are checked and fresh (else `DATA-LAYER-STALE`). A suspended country is never OK. Each verdict names Priority Mail International and lists the four layers; the box carries the no-marking and two-box notes when lithium is present. Results do not depend on item order. `tests/vectors/box.json` holds the 20 named box vectors from the plan, including the exact measured misread and a millivolt misread. Source: plan Task 13. Verified by `src/engine/verdict.test.ts` (13 tests), which failed before the module existed. The verified film fixture (`tests/fixtures/beats.json`) and its test wait for the exact film items.

### Changed

- Destination editions gain `personReviewedOn`: `reviewed: true` keeps meaning that the builder read every line on the live page and wrote our wording (on `checkedOn`); `personReviewedOn` records the date a person re-read every line against that wording, and stays `null` for Canada and the Philippines until that pass is done. The parser requires the field and refuses a value that is not a date. Source: orchestrator ruling on the Task 12 report (the desk pass becomes Stephen's Task 27). Verified by the new tests "records the person's review apart from the builder's read" and "keeps the person's desk pass separate", which failed first.
- How rule data changes are committed: a change to a part file in `rules/` and the regenerated `rules/edition.json` always land in the same commit. They are one logical change, because CI's `edition:check` fails any commit where the two disagree.
- `src/engine/edition.ts`: a rule id may now appear more than once when each entry asks a different question, so `DATA-NEEDS-ANSWER` can ask about alcohol, device condition and battery placement. An exact duplicate (same id and question) is still refused, and an unknown question is refused. Source: plan Task 11 (`DATA-NEEDS-ANSWER` carries an `askId`). Verified by the new test "lets one rule id ask different questions, but refuses an exact duplicate", which failed first.
- `.github/workflows/ci.yml`: the concurrency group now includes the commit SHA on pull requests too, so a newer push no longer cancels the run for the previous commit. Source: run 44236969420 for bd14d63 was cancelled by the next push, leaving that commit with no verdict. Verified: the YAML parses, and every PR commit after this one shows its own completed run.
- `rules/sources.json`: the USPS export source now cites the Publication 52 PDF and its printed issue date, August 2026, which supersedes the February 2026 edition. Source: plan Task 11 (record the Pub 52 edition from the PDF cover). Verified by reading the first two pages of `https://pe.usps.com/cpim/ftp/pubs/Pub52/pub52.pdf` live on 2026-11-17; edition rebuilt and `edition:check` passes.

- `rules/label-words.json`: "tobacco" and "cigarette" on a label now tag the item `tobacco`, and "cigar" tags it `cigar`, so the export rules can act on them. Source: plan Task 11 (tobacco is in the rule list; there is no tobacco category). Verified by the new test "reads tobacco and cigars from the label", which failed first.

### Fixed

- Alcoholic drinks and lighters now have their own tags. `alcoholic_drink` comes from the alcoholic-drinks category or a strength on the label such as "13.5% ALC" or "ABV"; `lighter` comes from the lighters category or the word "lighter". Canada's alcoholic-drinks line (CA-P-01) and lighters line (CA-P-02) match these tags, and so do the USPS alcoholic-drinks and matches-and-lighters rules. Before, CA-P-01 matched any label that mentioned alcohol, so a face cream with "alcohol denat." was taken out as an alcoholic drink, and CA-P-02 matched anything flammable, so nail polish listed "lighters" as its reason. A wine read as packaged food is now still an alcoholic drink. Source: second-model review (Fable), finding I4. Verified by four guard vectors (face cream, nail polish, wine read as food, lighter control), which failed first; two hand-built test fixtures gained the tags the engine now derives from their category.
- `src/engine/facts.ts` and `rules/usps-export.json`: the model's "alcohol on the label" flag alone no longer decides. It adds a weak `alcohol_flag` tag that asks the sender "Does the label list alcohol?", and their "no" clears it. Alcohol is still certain, and wins over a "no", when the label words show it (for example "alcohol denat."), when the category carries it (hand sanitizer, alcoholic drinks) or when the sender answers "yes". Before, a small model saying yes to "cetearyl alcohol" took the lotion out, and the sender could not undo it. Source: second-model review (Fable), finding I3. Verified by three guard vectors (flag alone asks, sender's "no" passes, "ALCOHOL DENAT." still takes out), which failed first; the two Task 9 alcohol tests now assert the new rule under their original names.
- Printed lithium grams now count (`src/engine/battery.ts`, `src/engine/types.ts`, `rules/usps-export.json`). Battery facts carry `lithiumGrams`; a printed value is battery evidence and marks the battery lithium metal. New rules: a cell above 1 g takes the item out (`USPS-INTL-LI-METAL-CELL-OVER`), a battery above 2 g takes it out (`USPS-INTL-LI-METAL-BATTERY-OVER`), and 1 to 2 g with an unknown cell count asks the cell question (`USPS-INTL-LI-METAL-CLASS`). "We can't confirm its lithium content" now applies only when no grams are printed. Before, grams were read and then ignored, so a 1.2 g cell was only "Check this" and a toy with 0.6 g printed had no battery facts at all. Source: second-model reviews, Codex M6 and Fable I11. Verified by five guard vectors (two take-outs, the cell question, a 0.5 g control that passes, grams as the only evidence), which failed first, and by the rule test that the gram thresholds equal the edition limits.
- `src/engine/battery.ts`: a battery number that is not a real number (text such as "150", NaN, a negative) now marks the read implausible, so the item is "Check this" with the "numbers don't agree" reason. Before, a typed fix that reached the engine as text passed the "above 0" test but failed every numeric limit, so a laptop with Wh typed as "150" was "OK to pack" instead of "Take it out". Source: second-model review (Fable), finding I1. Verified by two guard vectors (Wh as text, volts as nonsense), which failed first.
- `src/engine/battery.ts` and `rules/label-words.json`: label words now count as battery evidence even when the model misses the battery flag or picks the wrong category. "Power bank", "portable charger" or "battery pack" on the label makes the battery loose ("Take it out"), except on a phone, tablet, laptop, smartwatch or earbuds, whose own sticker often says "battery pack". "Li-ion", "lithium ion", "lithium polymer", "Li-Po" and "rechargeable" count as battery evidence, and the lithium-ion words set the chemistry. Before, a power bank read as other electronics could reach "OK to pack", and so could an RC car labelled "LI-ION BATTERY INSIDE" with the flag missed. Source: second-model review (Fable), finding C2b. Verified by `tests/vectors/box-guards.json` (run by `src/engine/guards.test.ts`): the two probes failed first, and a laptop "LI-ION BATTERY PACK" control that the first version of the fix wrongly took out now stays "OK to pack".
- Destination coverage: every listing line now either matches item tags or carries a recorded `noMatch` decision (`not_a_gift_item`, `exception_to_line`, `cannot_judge_from_photo` or `observation`) with a one-line reason, and the parser refuses a line with neither or both. Before, 24 Canada lines and 7 Philippines lines matched nothing and the destination layer still read "checked", so margarine could pass to Canada. New matches: margarine (CA-P-08, take out), vaping (CA-P-06, take out), gold or silver bullion (CA-P-07 and PH-P-02, take out), pet food and treats (CA-R-08, check), seed packets and live plants (PH-R-02, check), with label words for each; the e-cigarette category also carries `vaping`. Line hashes are unchanged. Source: second-model review (Codex), finding H1. Verified by the new tests "needs every listing line to match tags or record why it matches nothing", "gives every line a tag match or a recorded reason", "maps the food and gift lines that a sender could pack" and "reads the gift words the destination lines need" (shown to fail with the previous word list), plus six new destination vectors.
- `src/engine/edition.ts` and `src/engine/layers.ts`: dates must exist on the calendar (no month 13, no February 30), in the edition and in the date the caller passes as today. A layer whose check date is after today, or whose dates are not real, now reads "missing" instead of "checked". Before, an impossible date produced a not-a-number age and a future date a negative one, and both counted as fresh. Source: second-model review (Codex), finding M4. Verified by the new tests "rejects dates that do not exist on the calendar" and "never reads an impossible today or a check dated after today as fresh", which failed first.
- `src/engine/edition.ts`: the edition parser now refuses an edition with no USPS export item rules, and a reviewed country listing with no lines unless the listing says it gives no list. Before, either one loaded, and its layer still reported "checked" while checking nothing, so matches to the Philippines or a liquid to an emptied listing could pass. Source: second-model review (Codex), finding H3. Verified by the new tests "rejects an edition that has lost its export rules" and "rejects a reviewed listing with no lines, unless the listing gives no list", which failed first.
- `src/engine/battery.ts`: a power bank or a loose battery is now always read as loose ("Take it out"), even when an answer saved under an earlier reading says the battery is installed. Before, the saved answer was checked first, so a power bank (3.7 V, 2600 mAh, 9.62 Wh) whose category was corrected from "other electronics" could pass. Source: second-model review (Codex), finding H2. Verified by the new test "keeps a power bank or loose battery loose whatever a saved answer says", which failed first.
- `rules/label-words.json`: fatty alcohols that sit on most lotion labels (cetyl, cetearyl, stearyl, benzyl, behenyl, myristyl, lanolin, oleyl, lauryl, isostearyl, arachidyl and phenethyl alcohol) no longer tag an item as containing alcohol, which would have taken lotions out of the box. "Alcohol denat." and "SD alcohol" still do. Source: found while writing the USPS export rules (Task 11). Verified by the new test "does not read fatty alcohols in a lotion as alcohol" in `src/engine/facts.test.ts`, which failed before the change.
